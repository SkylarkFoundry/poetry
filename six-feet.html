By Kevin Kwilinski

Six Feet

We woke and gazed out,
seeing a violent stream now coursed,
rapidly between us and our neighbor's place.
 
A strid, deadly if crossed,
like that of which Wordsworth wrote,
recalling the force of prayer.
 
Strid from the Old English,
word "Stryth" meaning "turmoil",
recalling the force of water.
 
Strid for the deceptive possibility,
of easily striding across so narrow a gap,
recalling the force of hope.
 
Just six feet wide,
was Wordsworth's ancient strid,
in its ancient course past Bolton priory.
 
Just six feet wide,
but taking into its icy depths so many,
lads and ladies and lovers who dared to pass.
 
Just six feet of social distance,
but no less a thief of friendship,
than the six feet of an earthy grave.
 
"Strangled by a merciless force",
the priory cut off from the body,
leaving St. Mary and St. Cuthbert alone in prayer.
 
But Moses’ staff was lifted high,
and God parted the sea enough,
to consume the six foot width of Pharoah's chariot.
 
And God kept faith,
Delivering His people out of bondage,
by deftly closing that six foot gap.
 
And His Son kept faith,
Delivering His people out of bondage,
from six feet up on that old cross.
