By Kevin Kwilinski

Scale

If the mountains one day wake
from their ancient peaceful sleep,
then gaze upon our valley,
will they notice you and I
as arm in arm we dally?
 
We are to them, tiny specs
that breathe but for a second,
living in their shadow cast
by dark and ancient granite,
to them we scarcely last.
 
But our soul they’ll surely see,
since it lives eternally
and makes their own life feel brief,
as rain erodes brittle cliff
and wears down their tallest reef.
 
Then our soul will smile back
upon their grandeur rising,
not because of temporal arch,
just dancing in that beauty,
whistling on its heaven march.
